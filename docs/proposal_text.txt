The platform aims to eliminate fragmented processes, improve operational efficiency, enhance service delivery, strengthen institutional governance, and support evidence-based decision-making through real-time access to reliable information. Following extensive stakeholder consultations, departmental interviews, workflow observations, document reviews, and operational audits, it became evident that many institutional processes are performed manually or through disconnected systems. Critical activities such as student admissions, academic administration, financial management, human resource operations, procurement, quality assurance, research management, and communication rely heavily on paper-based procedures, spreadsheets, and isolated databases. These approaches have resulted in duplicated data, delays in approvals, inconsistencies in reporting, limited transparency, and increased operational costs. The proposed ERP platform seeks to address these challenges by establishing a unified digital ecosystem where all institutional departments operate on a shared database while maintaining appropriate security controls and role-based access. Information entered once into the system becomes available, subject to authorization, across all relevant departments, thereby eliminating repetitive data entry and improving institutional coordination. Unlike conventional student management systems that focus primarily on admissions and examination processing, the proposed solution has been designed as a full Enterprise Resource Planning System. It supports the complete institutional lifecycle, beginning with prospective student recruitment and admissions, continuing through academic delivery, financial management, quality assurance, research, human resource administration, procurement, alumni engagement, and extending to community outreach programs and post-graduation services. The platform has also been designed to support TICH's unique operational model, which includes centralized administration at the main campus, decentralized community colleges operating across multiple sub-counties, competency-based education and training (CBET), nursing block training, work-study programs, SACCO services, donor-funded projects, and regulatory compliance with Kenyan higher education authorities. To maximize accessibility and operational efficiency, the ERP will be delivered through multiple digital channels. These include a public institutional website, secure web-based management portals, dedicated student and staff portals, a mobile application developed using Flutter, a desktop application and RESTful APIs that facilitate secure communication between the various system components. The platform will additionally incorporate automated notifications through email and SMS, AI-powered virtual assistance, secure payment gateway integration, and advanced analytics dashboards for institutional leadership. Security, scalability, maintainability, and regulatory compliance constitute the architectural foundation of the proposed solution. The platform will implement Role-Based Access Control (RBAC), Multi-Factor Authentication (MFA), encrypted communications, comprehensive audit logging, automated backups, disaster recovery mechanisms, and compliance with the Kenya Data Protection Act, internationally recognized information security principles, and institutional governance policies. Ultimately, the implementation of the TICH ERP Platform represents an institutional transformation program that modernizes business processes, enhances accountability, strengthens collaboration across departments, improves service delivery for students and staff, and establishes a sustainable digital foundation capable of supporting the institution's long-term strategic growth. 1.1 Background of TICH TICH is a higher education institution committed to delivering quality education, vocational training, community-based development programs, research, and professional capacity building. Over the years, the institution has expanded its academic offerings, administrative functions, partnerships, and community outreach initiatives, resulting in increasingly complex operational processes that require efficient coordination across multiple departments. The institution currently comprises several functional divisions, including Administration, Academics, Finance, Human Resources, Quality Assurance, Procurement, Research, Student Affairs, Information and Communication Technology (ICT), Community Colleges, SACCO operations, and executive management. Each department performs specialized responsibilities while relying on information generated by other departments to accomplish institutional objectives. As student enrollment continues to increase &amp; expected to increase and institutional programs diversify, existing manual processes have become increasingly difficult to manage. Many operational activities involve repetitive paperwork, multiple approval stages, manual reconciliation of financial transactions, spreadsheet-based reporting, and duplicated data entry across departments. These practices increase the likelihood of human error, reduce operational efficiency, and make it difficult for management to obtain timely and accurate information for strategic decision-making. A comprehensive requirement gathering exercise involving interviews, departmental workshops, workflow analysis, and operational observations was conducted to understand the institution's existing business processes, operational challenges, approval hierarchies, regulatory obligations, and long-term strategic goals. The findings confirmed the need for an integrated enterprise platform capable of managing all institutional operations through a centralized and secure digital environment. The proposed ERP platform has therefore been designed specifically around TICH's verified operational workflows rather than adopting generic higher education software. Every functional module, approval process, business rule, and user role has been modelled based on actual institutional practices observed during the requirement gathering exercise. This ensures that the final system aligns with existing operational realities while introducing automation, standardization, accountability, and opportunities for future institutional growth. By implementing the TICH ERP Platform, the institution will establish a unified digital infrastructure that supports efficient service delivery, promotes transparency, strengthens regulatory compliance, improves data quality, enhances collaboration among departments, and provides management with real-time insights necessary for effective planning and decision-making. 1.2 Problem Statement The rapid growth of TICH in academic programs, decentralized community colleges, research initiatives, and institutional support services has significantly increased the complexity of managing daily operations. While the institution has continued to expand its educational and administrative activities, many of its core business processes remain largely dependent on manual procedures, paper-based documentation, spreadsheets, and isolated software tools such as quickbook and workpay that operate independently of one another. The comprehensive requirement-gathering exercise conducted across the institution—including interviews with senior management, departmental heads, administrative personnel, finance officers, lecturers, quality assurance personnel, ICT staff, and students—revealed several operational inefficiencies that negatively affect institutional performance, service delivery, decision-making, and regulatory compliance. One of the most significant challenges identified is the fragmentation of institutional data. Student records, staff information, financial transactions, academic records, procurement activities, quality assurance documents, and administrative reports are maintained in different locations using separate files and manual registers. As a result, departments frequently duplicate information, spend considerable time verifying records, and experience inconsistencies when generating institutional reports. The admissions process similarly relies on multiple manual verification stages involving several departments. Applicant information is reviewed independently by academic staff, administration, and finance before admission decisions are finalized. Payment verification often requires manual confirmation, while admission letters and registration numbers are generated through separate processes. These disconnected procedures increase processing time, create opportunities for human error, and reduce the overall efficiency of student onboarding. Academic administration presents additional operational challenges. Curriculum planning, course creation, unit allocation, timetable preparation, lesson planning, examination moderation, and student progression management require extensive coordination among the Academic Registrar, Heads of Department, tutors, and the Academic Board. Under the current operational model, much of this coordination depends on manual communication, spreadsheets, and printed documentation, making it difficult to maintain consistency, manage curriculum changes efficiently, and prevent scheduling conflicts involving lecturers, classrooms, and students. Financial management is similarly affected by fragmented workflows. Student fee structures, invoice generation, payment verification, financial clearance, procurement approvals, payroll processing, statutory deductions, budgeting, and financial reporting are managed through multiple independent processes that require frequent manual reconciliation. Delays in payment verification directly affect course registration, examination eligibility, graduation clearance, and institutional reporting. Furthermore, the absence of real-time integration between financial transactions and student records limits the institution's ability to provide immediate financial visibility to both management and students. Human Resource operations also rely heavily on manual administration. Employee onboarding, contract management, leave administration, payroll preparation, statutory compliance, staff performance monitoring, and professional development records are maintained through independent processes that require repeated data entry and manual tracking. This increases administrative workload while limiting management's ability to monitor workforce performance and contractual obligations efficiently. The institution's decentralized community college model introduces additional operational complexity. Training centres distributed across multiple sub-counties require synchronized admissions, student registration, attendance tracking, academic monitoring, and communication with the central campus. Without an integrated digital platform, maintaining consistent academic standards and operational visibility across geographically distributed centers becomes increasingly difficult. Quality Assurance and Procurement departments experience similar challenges. Internal quality audits, compliance monitoring, supplier verification, budget approvals, procurement workflows, and institutional policy reviews are largely coordinated through manual documentation and email communication. These approaches reduce transparency, complicate audit preparation, and make it difficult to monitor the status of institutional compliance activities in real time. Communication across the institution is another significant concern. Students, lecturers, administrators, and management often rely on separate communication channels to receive announcements, timetable updates, fee reminders, examination notifications, and administrative directives. The absence of a centralized communication platform increases the risk of delayed information dissemination and inconsistent messaging. Institutional reporting also presents considerable challenges. Executive management requires timely access to accurate information regarding student enrolment, academic performance, financial status, staff performance, procurement activities, research projects, and quality assurance indicators. However, because operational data is distributed across multiple systems and manual records, report generation is time-consuming and often requires extensive manual compilation before meaningful insights can be obtained. Security and regulatory compliance represent another area requiring improvement. The institution manages highly sensitive student, staff, financial, medical, and academic information that must be protected against unauthorized access, accidental modification, and data loss. Existing manual processes provide limited mechanisms for audit trails, role-based access control, data encryption, and comprehensive activity monitoring, increasing institutional risk while making compliance with the Kenya Data Protection Act and other regulatory requirements more challenging. Collectively, these operational challenges reduce institutional efficiency, increase administrative costs, delay service delivery, and limit management's ability to make informed strategic decisions. They also create unnecessary workloads for staff while affecting the overall experience of students and external stakeholders. The implementation of an integrated Enterprise Resource Planning (ERP) Platform is therefore necessary to consolidate institutional operations within a single secure information system. The proposed solution will automate critical workflows, centralize institutional data, standardize operational procedures, strengthen security, improve communication, enhance regulatory compliance, and provide real-time visibility into all major institutional activities. By addressing the identified challenges through a unified digital platform, TICH will establish a scalable technological foundation capable of supporting its continued institutional growth while improving efficiency, accountability, and service delivery across all departments. 1.3 Summary of Key Challenges Identified The requirement-gathering exercise identified the following major institutional challenges that the proposed ERP platform is designed to address: Fragmentation of Institutional Data: Student records, financial transactions, staff data, and procurement activities are currently managed in isolated databases, spreadsheets, and manual registers. This fragmentation leads to possible duplicated data, increased time spent verifying records, and can lead significant inconsistencies when generating institutional reports. Inefficient, Manual Operational Workflows: Core processes such as student admissions, academic administration, and financial management mostly rely on paper-based documentation and manual approvals. These manual touchpoints delay service delivery, create opportunities for human error, and increase the administrative workload for staff. Limited Management Visibility: Because operational data is not centralised, executive management lacks real-time insights into institutional performance, such as enrollment trends, financial status, or procurement activities. Report generation is currently a time-consuming, compilation process. Inadequate Security and Regulatory Compliance: Existing processes offer limited mechanisms for audit trails, role-based access control, and data encryption. This increases institutional risk and makes maintaining compliance with the Kenya Data Protection Act and other regulatory requirements particularly challenging. Communication Silos: The lack of a centralized platform forces students and staff to rely on disjointed channels for critical updates, such as timetable changes, fee reminders, and administrative directives, leading to delayed or inconsistent information dissemination. Financial Reconciliation Delays: Financial management is hindered by multiple independent processes for invoicing, payment verification, and fee clearance, which require frequent and time-consuming manual reconciliation. Academic Coordination Hurdles: The coordination of curriculum planning, unit allocation, and timetable preparation relies on manual communication and spreadsheets, which may make it difficult to prevent scheduling conflicts for students and lecturers. Fragmented Student Admissions: The admissions process involves multiple manual verification stages across different departments, which may create bottlenecks and increases the time required for student onboarding. Operational Transparency Gaps: Many quality assurance, procurement, and policy review activities are coordinated through informal channels like email, which reduces overall transparency and complicates preparation for institutional audits. HR Administration Burdens: Human Resource tasks such as employee onboarding, contract management, and performance monitoring are maintained through independent, repetitive processes that limit management's ability to monitor workforce performance effectively. Delayed Financial Visibility: The lack of real-time integration between financial transactions and student records prevents management and students from accessing immediate, accurate financial information. SECTION 2: PROJECT OBJECTIVES The successful implementation of an Enterprise Resource Planning (ERP) platform requires clearly defined objectives that align technological solutions with institutional goals. The objectives outlined in this proposal are derived directly from the findings of the requirement-gathering exercise and reflect the operational, academic, administrative, financial, and strategic needs of TICH. The proposed ERP platform is designed not merely to automate existing manual processes, but to transform institutional operations through integrated digital technologies, standardized workflows, improved governance, enhanced security, and data-driven decision-making. The objectives therefore focus on improving efficiency, accountability, collaboration, regulatory compliance, and service delivery across every functional area of the institution. 2.1 General Objective The primary objective of this project is to design, develop, implement, and deploy a secure, scalable, and fully integrated Higher Education Enterprise Resource Planning (ERP) Platform that centralizes and automates the academic, administrative, financial, human resource, research, procurement, quality assurance, and operational functions of TICH while supporting institutional growth, regulatory compliance, and evidence-based decision-making. The ERP platform will provide a unified digital environment through which students, staff, administrators, management, and external stakeholders can securely access the services relevant to their roles, thereby improving institutional efficiency, operational transparency, and overall service delivery. 2.3 Strategic Objectives To achieve the overall project vision, the implementation will pursue the following strategic objectives: 1. Digital Transformation Transform TICH from a predominantly manual and paper-based operational environment into a fully integrated digital institution where business processes are automated, standardized, and centrally managed. 2. Institutional Integration Integrate all major institutional departments into a single centralized information system that enables seamless sharing of information while maintaining strict security and role-based access controls. 3. Operational Efficiency Reduce administrative workload by automating repetitive tasks, accelerating approval workflows, and eliminating unnecessary manual processes. 4. Data-Driven Decision Making Provide executive management with real-time dashboards, analytics, and institutional performance indicators to support strategic planning, monitoring, and policy development. 5. Regulatory Compliance Ensure that all institutional operations comply with the Kenya Data Protection Act, Ministry of Education requirements, TVETA guidelines, Nursing Council regulations, KRA statutory requirements, and other applicable legal and regulatory frameworks. 6. Institutional Growth Develop a scalable technology platform capable of supporting future institutional expansion, including additional campuses, community colleges, academic programs, mobile services, and external system integrations. It specifies the business processes, institutional departments, user groups, system modules, technologies, integrations, deployment environments, and implementation activities that will be included in the project, while also identifying functions that are intentionally excluded from the current implementation phase. The scope has been developed directly from the verified findings obtained during the requirement-gathering exercise. Every module, workflow, approval process, and user interaction included in this proposal reflects actual institutional operations observed across the Administration, Academics, Finance, Human Resource, Procurement, Quality Assurance, Community Colleges, ICT, Student Affairs, and Executive Management departments. The objective of defining a comprehensive project scope is to establish a common understanding among all project stakeholders regarding the expected deliverables, implementation boundaries, and responsibilities throughout the project lifecycle. 3.2 Functional Scope The ERP platform shall automate and integrate the following institutional business functions. Departments and Modules 1.      Administration Department Institutional Budgeting &amp; Strategic Planning Multi-Tiered Budget Configuration: System supports the creation, coordination, and oversight of institutional planning activities categorized across annual, monthly, and weekly timelines. Cross-Departmental Financial Aggregation: Consolidates monthly and annual budget requisitions pulled directly from all departments, including Finance, HR, Procurement, Infrastructure, Academics, Communications, ICT, Research &amp; Monitoring and Evaluation, Networking and Marketing, and the SACCO. Strict Requisition Deadlines: Features built-in system constraints to prevent late submissions, mandating that all monthly departmental plans and budget requests must be presented by the middle of the prior month. Curriculum System Adaptability: Provides native architectural support for planning budgets under Competency-Based Education (CBE) frameworks alongside traditional educational systems. Automated Approval Pipeline: Integrates a structured workflow for plan verification and final fiscal clearance: Departments submit plans to the Administration interface. Plans are automatically routed to the Finance Department to verify the availability of sufficient funds. Validated plans are escalated to the Executive dashboard (CEO) for final dual-authorization and signature by the TICH CEO and the Chairperson of the TICH-TRUST. Digitized Fund Distribution: Upon executive approval, the system triggers a fund release command through Finance, allowing Administration Department to digitally withdraw and distribute allocations to respective departments on a monthly basis. Student Admissions &amp; Lifecycle Onboarding Dual-Access Application Portal: Powers a fully online admission framework featuring multi-step digital forms (capturing personal information, academic history, supporting documents, and program choices) accessible by external applicants or processed internally by the Admin office. Onboarding &amp; Bulk Enrollment: Includes robust bulk enrollment utilities and self-service onboarding portals to process large student cohorts efficiently. Digital Document Intake Checklist: Enforces mandatory data and document uploads during student onboarding, securely archiving: Full legal name and two colored passport photographs. KCSE result slips or vocational qualifications (e.g., Class 8 certificates). National ID copies or official Birth Certificates. Digital receipt or proof of application fee payment. Automated Admission Lifecycle Workflow: Directs the progressive phases of student intake: Step 1: Applicant submits the application, and the system instantly generates a Unique Application ID. Step 2: The Academic Department reviews and verifies the qualifications within their dashboard. Step 3: Upon academic approval, the system issues payment instructions where the Unique Application ID serves as the student's reference/account number for the application fee. Step 4: Finance verifies payment manually, or the system automatically switches the status to "Paid" via real-time M-Pesa integration. Step 5: Upon payment confirmation, the Administration Department generates the official admission letter, and the system produces a permanent Student Registration Number. Structured Admission Letter Generation: Automatically compiles and dispatches a comprehensive admission package to the student detailing: Core course of study, chosen center of study, and official institutional reporting date. Structured Registration Numbers: Formats codes based on campus parameters, such as THCM/N/J13-03/2026 (for Community Colleges utilizing course, exam body, month abbreviations, numbers, county codes, and year) or THCM/J13/2026. Automated Document Attachments: Automatically appends relevant course-specific fee structures, institutional bank details, general enrollment requirements, and academically approved medical forms. Compliance, Legal &amp; Regulatory Oversight Statutory Details Repository: Centralizes and tracks the institutional compliance files, registration documents, and legal certifications required by oversight bodies. Regulatory Alignment Alerts: Monitors and evaluates operational alignment with the Ministry of Education, Kenya Revenue Authority (KRA), Public Health mandates, and technical examining bodies such as TVET and TVETA. Inspection Readiness Dashboard: Ensures all mandatory compliance documents are perpetually available online to facilitate seamless, on-demand physical or digital inspections by external legal and educational authorities. Cashflow &amp; Procurement Management Oversight Procurement-to-Pay Lifecycle Tracking: Provides administrative visibility over the institutional supply chain workflow: Procurement identifies, vets, and approves an appropriate supplier. The supplier delivers goods or renders services and submits an invoice to Finance. Finance routes the invoice back to Procurement for absolute verification against delivery parameters. Three-Way Match Verification: Enables Procurement to confirm that physical goods or services match invoice details perfectly before returning the verified file to Finance; inaccurate invoices are automatically rejected back to the supplier. Ledger Synchronization: Once Finance executes payment via authorized digital methods (e.g., bank transfer or M-Pesa), the transaction is pushed directly into QuickBooks for synchronized financial reporting, audit trailing, and bank reconciliation. Auxiliary Services &amp; SACCO Facilities Operations SACCO Housing Integration: Integrates with the TICH SACCO data layer to manage institutional student hostels. Hostel Awareness &amp; Onboarding: Automatically flags hostel availability to incoming students during the initial online admission process. Automated Boarding Ledger: Manages monthly recurring accommodation billings (configured at KES 3,500) and tracks individual student rent payments. Cafeteria Operations Ledger: Manages the operational parameters of the SACCO-run cafeteria, including tracking staff memberships and maintaining distinct accounting separation through a dedicated, isolated bank account.   2. Academics The platform implements a role-based access management structure to isolate and enforce institutional oversight, policy updates, curriculum approvals, and resource scheduling across physical and virtual environments. Academic Registrar Management Terminal The Academic Registrar Management Terminal serves as the central control module for back-office student administration, catalog mapping, institutional scheduling, and student registration tracking. It executes administrative lifecycle logic and ensures data consistency across the university network. 1. Academic Calendar Configuration Engine Dynamic Framework Curation: The terminal houses a dynamic, flexible academic calendar builder designed to accommodate structural shifts in enrollment windows, holiday breaks, and practical field placement blocks. The Primary Trimester Model: The primary instance of the calendar engine enforces an academic year structure broken down into exactly 3 active terms/semesters per calendar year which can be changed if need arises. Intake Synchronization: This scheduling engine directly synchronizes with the core institutional intakes framework to coordinate admission workflows for the cohorts e.g. January, May, and September cohorts, also specified by the Academic registrar 2. Student Lifecycle Administration Engine This engine routes, processes, and archives student-initiated lifecycle modifications: Waitlist Management Logs: Automates student pipeline traffic by managing waiting lists, analyzing candidate distributions, and transitioning applicants to active status as enrollment spaces open. Deferral Request Queues: Evaluates and stores formal applications from students seeking to pause their studies, dynamically preserving their registration tokens across future terms while triggering financial pauses in the ledger. Recognition of Prior Learning (RPL) &amp; Credit Transfers: Features a specialized ingestion interface to evaluate alternative entry paths and calculate transfer credits. It converts documented practical trades, work histories, or Community Health Provider (CHP) experience into accredited academic credit equivalents matching vocational frameworks. 4. Credential Verification &amp; Exam The Registrar coordinates the ultimate validation checks required to clear students for summative testing sequences: Roster Compilation: Processes data arrays to compile final, verified student rosters per active unit/module, serving as the baseline data for attendance logs. Exam Eligibility Matrix Loops: Runs a mandatory gatekeeping check across active student records before generating testing clearances: Class Attendance Validation: Verifies that the student's digitized check-in history satisfies a minimum threshold of exactly 90% attendance across the unit duration. Fee Clearance Evaluation: Scans financial data tables to confirm an absolute Zero Balance status on tuition and institutional ledgers. Official Examination Card Distribution: Generates and distributes signed digital exam cards directly to compliant student portal profiles only after the record passes both the attendance and fee gates. Allocation Oversight Engine: Monitors contact hour distributions across faculty profiles. It cross-references active room schedules, lab usage parameters, and library asset bookings to prevent resource overallocation or calendar overlap. Cap Safeguard Thresholds: Automatically flags individual instructor profiles if cumulative assignments exceed weekly contact limits, alerting leadership to adjust assignments. 3. Departmental Segregation Matrix (The Three-Core Framework) The platform isolates system controls, curriculum parameters, and diagnostic tools across three explicit departmental profiles: Profile 1: Community Health and Development (CHD) Regulatory Compliance Mapping: Manages educational tracks and lesson configurations aligned with the Curriculum Development Assessment and Certification Council (CDACC) and Technical and Vocational Education and Training (TVET) frameworks. It enforces statutory requirements from the Nursing Council across three core tracks: General Nursing, Midwifery, and Community Health Nursing. It locks student progression until clinical field logs and skills laboratory assessment marks match passing rules. Profile 2: Vocational Training Artisan Specialty Allocation: Coordinates competency tracking matrices across five distinct artisan specialties: Masonry Electrical Installation Welding Home Care Management (Community Colleges) Community Colleges Network NITA Framework Integration: Uses the National Industrial Training Authority (NITA) modular framework rules. Instead of using traditional credit-hour metrics, the dashboard provides instructors with digital skill checklists to log practical competency development. Decentralized Cell Monitoring: Connects with the Affiliated Artisans Registry to track mentor assignments and practical field logs submitted from remote sub-county learning sites. Profile 3: Information Communication Technology (ICT) Internal Software Catalog Management: Oversees internal technical certification paths, specifically governing the curriculum layers for the specialized Data Science track across Certificate and Diploma levels. D. Teaching Staff (Trainers &amp; Tutors) Workspace This section defines the technical specifications and functional workflows for the shared instructional workspace. Designed as a unified interface, this workspace provides operational tools for active Trainers and Tutors while granting real-time monitoring, audit, and intervention capabilities to Heads of Departments (HODs) across both physical classrooms and virtual learning environments. 1. Lesson Plan Submission &amp; Approval Pipeline To maintain instructional quality across all departments, the platform enforces a digital state machine for lesson curation before any class session is initiated. Structural Objective Compilation: Tutors use a standardized digital template to input specific lesson objectives, planned topics, core competencies targeted, and total contact hours per module. HOD Approval Dashboard: Upon submission, the lesson plan is routed to the respective HOD's authorization terminal. HODs can review, edit, comment, or approve plans directly within the interface. Approved plans automatically clear the associated unit on the master academic timetable. Historical Audit Repository: All approved and modified lesson plans are indexed in a read-only database section accessible to the Academic Registrar to track institutional curriculum coverage. 2. Classroom Delivery &amp; Environmental Interfaces The ERP connects classroom tools with physical and digital assets to simplify delivery tracking for both instructors and departmental heads. Virtual Learning System (VLS) Integration: Provides single-sign-on (SSO) buttons to launch online lectures, track student login durations, and sync recorded video sessions back to the Student Portal. Physical Asset &amp; Space Registry Hooks: Automatically cross-references physical timetables to reserve resources like the Central Library, specialized equipment hubs, and the ICT Computer Lab during scheduled class hours. HOD Workload Allocation Monitor: Feeds real-time delivery data into the departmental dashboard, tracking actual contact hours against assigned weekly targets to ensure balanced lecturer workloads. 3. Attendance Verification To protect the integrity of the institution's exam eligibility gates, the platform uses a dual-layer verification process for student attendance logs. The department ensures that all purchases are conducted transparently, competitively, and in accordance with institutional procurement policies and financial controls. The requirement-gathering exercise established that procurement activities involve requisition management, supplier sourcing, quotation evaluation, supplier selection, invoice verification, payment processing, goods receipt, inventory management, and asset tracking. The proposed ERP system shall automate and integrate these processes to improve accountability, efficiency, compliance, and reporting. Requisition 1. Requisition Initiation The procurement process begins when a department identifies a need for goods, services, equipment, or supplies. The requesting department shall: Create a procurement requisition. Specify the required items or services. Indicate quantities required. Provide justification for the request. Submit the requisition for approval. The system shall generate a unique requisition number for tracking purposes. 2. Requisition Review and Approval Upon submission, the requisition shall undergo the institution's approval process. The system shall support: Departmental approval workflows. Budget availability verification. Management approvals where required. Requisition status tracking. Only approved requisitions shall proceed to supplier sourcing. 3.Supplier Quotation Collection After approval of the requisition, the Procurement Department shall invite quotations from multiple suppliers. The procurement team shall: Request quotations from qualified suppliers. Receive quotation documents. Record supplier details. Upload supplier quotations into the system. Maintain a history of all submitted quotations. The system shall allow attachment and storage of quotation documents for future reference and auditing purposes. 4. Procurement Committee Evaluation The requirement-gathering exercise established that supplier selection is conducted through a Procurement Committee. The committee shall: Review all submitted quotations. Compare supplier pricing. Evaluate product quality. Assess supplier reliability. Consider delivery timelines. Verify supplier compliance requirements. The system shall support: Quotation comparison. Supplier evaluation scoring. Committee recommendations.  Approval documentation. 5. Supplier Selection After evaluation, the Procurement Committee shall identify the most suitable supplier. The system shall: Record the selected supplier. Document reasons for selection. Maintain records of unsuccessful quotations. 6. Invoice Verification Once a supplier has been selected, the supplier shall issue an invoice. The Procurement Department shall verify that: The invoice corresponds to the approved requisition. Quantities match the requisition. Prices match the approved quotation. Approved procurement procedures were followed. The ERP system shall perform a three-way verification process involving: Approved Requisition. Approved Supplier Quotation. Supplier Invoice. 7. Payment Processing After successful invoice verification, the procurement department shall forward the approved invoice to the Finance Department for payment processing. The system shall: Route verified invoices to Finance. Track payment approval status. Record payment references. Update procurement records once payment is completed. Procurement personnel shall be able to monitor the payment status without requiring direct intervention from Finance.  8. Delivery of Goods or Services After payment approval, the supplier shall deliver the goods or services. The delivery may occur through: Supplier delivery to the institution. Collection of goods by authorized procurement personnel. Upon receipt of goods, the Procurement Department shall verify: Correct quantities delivered. Correct specifications supplied. Acceptable quality standards. Compliance with the approved order. The system shall record: Delivery confirmation. Date of receipt. Receiving officer. Delivery documentation. Any shortages or damages identified. The supplier shall provide a delivery note upon delivery. The system shall: Store delivery note records. Link delivery notes to the original requisition. Link delivery notes to supplier invoices. Link delivery notes to supplier quotations. Maintain a complete procurement audit trail. The system shall support supplier management by maintaining: Supplier profiles. Supplier contacts.. Supplier compliance documents. Supplier quotation history. Asset Management Requirements The requirement-gathering exercise established that the Procurement Department is responsible for managing institutional assets. The ERP system shall therefore include an Asset Management component Asset Registration When assets are acquired, the system shall: Assign a unique asset number. Record asset details. Record acquisition cost. Record supplier information. Record acquisition date. Record department ownership. Record asset location. Asset Tracking The system shall support: Asset movement tracking. Departmental asset allocation. Asset transfers. Asset maintenance history. Asset disposal records. Management shall be able to determine the location and status of any institutional asset at any time. 3.Inventory Management  The Procurement Department also manages institutional inventory and stores. Stock Monitoring The system shall: Track current stock levels. Monitor minimum stock thresholds. Generate low-stock alerts. Monitor stock movement history. Support periodic stock audits. This will help prevent stock shortages and improve inventory control 9.      TICH SACCO  The TICH Savings and Credit Cooperative Society (SACCO) is an internal department established to provide financial services and support welfare initiatives for members of staff.
